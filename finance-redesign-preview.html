Where the money goes

The recurring nut — what the house costs to own and keep running, whether anyone visits or not. These are split by ownership, not by who uses it.

Reserve fund the sinking fund for big-ticket jobs

An old house doesn't fail on a schedule. Both households pay in a fixed amount each year so a new roof or a rotted sill comes out of a pot that's already there — not a surprise bill split in a panic.

Balance, end of

Settling up

Everyone fronts different operating bills through the year. This nets them back to a clean 50/50. Capital projects don't appear here — they come out of the reserve, which both households already fund equally — and personal costs (a household's own propane on their week) don't count either.

Usage pot consumables, shared by how much each household uses the house

Fixed costs split evenly; the day-to-day consumables shouldn't. Each bed-night contributes to a small pot, so the household that's there all summer carries more of the wear-and-tear.

The house over time every year on record

Carrying costs creep up; capital comes in lumps. Seeing both together is how you know when to raise the reserve contribution before it runs dry.
